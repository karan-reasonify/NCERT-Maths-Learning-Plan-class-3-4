## 4. Why the 22 tools are not repeats of each other

- **Sorting** appears three times, each on a different rule: Tool 3 sorts by a number the child has just counted (flat faces, straight edges); Tool 5 asks one yes or no question of each everyday object (stamp); Tool 6 puts solids in circles by a rule and meets the overlap (a solid in two sets).
- **Dot grids** are drawn on in Tools 7 (triangular dots, a cube), 11 (right angles), 12 (acute and obtuse), 15 (shapes by right angles), 19 (shapes by number of angles) and 20 (cuts). In 11 and 12 drawing is one part of the tool, not the tool; in 15, 19 and 20 the question each asks of the drawing is different (kinds of angles, number of angles, pieces).
- **Push test** appears in Tools 13 and 14 for a learning reason: 13 asks whether the shape changes (rigid or not), 14 asks what happens to the angles (right becomes acute and obtuse), using Tool 12's words.
- **Identify a solid** appears in Tool 8 (from clues about parts) and Tool 9 (from a net or its pieces): the clue is different and so is the action (pick or build against fold).
- **Circle**: Tool 16 (every point is the same distance from the centre), 17 (diameter, double the radius), 18 (apply it: carpet, wheels, compass).
- **Counting**: Tool 3 (parts of a solid), 7 (hidden cubes), 10 (angles), 21 (shapes in a web). Different objects and different mistakes (M_SH_3, M_SH_6, M_AN_1, M_PU_1).
- Every tool follows a different object: blocks, solids, cubes, straws, angles, circles, matchsticks, webs.

## 5. The cap arithmetic and the user's choice

- 109 questions at 6 per tool need at least 19 tools if every question sat on a tool (109 / 6 = 18.2, so 19). There are 22 tools, with 103 questions on tools (about 4.7 each) and 6 on one image. 22 is not 19 because the tools follow purposes, not capacity: four tools are small by nature (Tool 4 has 3 questions, Tool 7 has 3, Tool 8 has 2, Tool 22 has 3) and each still costs a full media step before it.
- The cap forces three things: Tool 2 has 7 questions, and the push test (Tools 13 and 14) has 14. Nothing else exceeds 6. Tool 1 (6), Tool 12 (6) and Tool 15 (6) are exactly at the cap. C1 (11 questions) already has to be split between Tool 1 and an image, C5 (10) between Tools 5 and 6, and C24 (7) between Tools 15 and 19.

**(a) Keep the cap of 6 (no one changes anything).** Tool 2 gives cube_prism to Tool 3 (6 questions). The push test becomes three tools: Push the Triangle (straw_tri_make, straw_tri_push, straw_tri_trace, straw_tri_angles: 4), Push the Rectangle and Square (straw_rect_make, straw_rect_angles, straw_rect_push, straw_rect_after, straw_square, straw_compare: 6) and Push the Pentagon (pentagon_make, pentagon_q1, pentagon_q2, pentagon_q3: 4). Result: 23 tools and one more media item before Push the Pentagon. The third tool repeats the first two, and exists only to fit the cap. I do not recommend it.

**(b) Raise the cap to 7 for this chapter (recommended; the table in `tool_map.md`).** One number changes, for one concept. Tool 2 keeps cube_prism (7), Tools 13 and 14 hold 7 each. 22 tools. A cap of 8 changes nothing in this table (only Tool 1 could also take project1, leaving 5 on the image). A cap of 10 would let Tools 5 and 6 merge and a cap of 14 would let Tools 13 and 14 merge; both would make tools that do two things, so I do not recommend either.

**(c) Keep the cap of 6 and leave two items as teaching text (also 22 tools).** straw_tri_trace ("trace the triangles in your notebook", a notebook task) and straw_compare ("how are the angles of triangles and rectangles similar or different?", an open discussion) are changed from questions to talk items (a decision in `content.py` that only the analyst step and the user can make). Then Tool 13 has 6 and Tool 14 has 6. Together with cube_prism on Tool 3, every tool is at 6 or fewer and the table stays as written. The cost is two book items that no longer have a graded answer on a tool.

Other look-and-think items that could be called teaching text (they change no tool count): project1, craft1, the four "name objects in your classroom" prompts (angles_do2, right_objects, acute_obj, obtuse_obj).

**If the user wants fewer than 22 tools** (in the order I would give them up): (1) Tool 18 becomes an image of the carpet and the wheels with its 4 questions on the image (21 tools; the compass flower, the marking and the measuring are no longer done); (2) Tools 5 and 6 become one (needs cap 10); (3) Tools 13 and 14 become one (needs cap 14). I would keep all 22.

## 7. Where the brief and the flow rules pull apart (and how I expressed it)

- **Puzzle or activity videos (brief D):** the flow has only a hook and concept videos. The puzzles (C8 and C23, C19, C21, C22) are concept videos told as stories (Diksha's guessing game, Diksha's hidden shapes, Squiggly and Wiggly, Wiggly's matchsticks), and the puzzle itself is solved on the tool. Rows 16, 40, 42 and 44.
- **A tool for every meaningful objective against the flow:** every tool after Tool 1 needs a concept video or an image first, so each tool costs a media item: 21 media items before tools. This is why the four small tools matter and why I merged C6 and C7, C8 and C23.
- **Chapter revision with concept checks and application questions (brief E):** the review has no tool and no book item (the book has no end exercise), so it has no place for a checked question. It can only recap what was taught. I did not invent one.
- **Phase 1 and Phase 3 of a tool:** Tool 1 follows the hook, which teaches nothing, so its Phase 1 reconnects with the situation (Diksha's blocks), not a concept. Its Phase 3 is the opener's six questions, unusually many for an exploration tool (open question 12).
- **Exact book wording against the screen:** many of the 109 questions are "write the answer" prompts; the child answers by choosing, placing or drawing, and the book's words stay as the prompt. The book prints no answers; the answers I assume are the analyst's readings (analysis.md, Appendix A) and need sign-off.
- **Printed ambiguities that reach a tool:** E1 (nets A and C of p9 Q4 do not fold: Tool 9), E5 (clue f of p8 fits a cube and a cuboid: Tool 8), E7 (border5 unclear: Tool 5), E9 (dot-grid angles read by measure: Tool 11), E22 (several shapes fit one clue of p23 Q9: Tool 15), E21 (pentagonal prism never shown: Tool 8).
- **"Do not assign a teaching role to a classroom teacher":** six teacher notes and "Ask your teacher to help you use a compass" (Tool 18). Re-worded as facts for the child; the compass on screen needs no helper.
- **Measurement and estimation "where covered":** Tool 17 (measure a crease with a virtual thread), Tool 18 (compare wheels), Tool 16 (equal distance with a scale). No units and no estimation task were added.
- **Class 4 calculations:** only F+V−E (Tool 3) and counts. No arithmetic was added.
- **Material tasks:** straws and clay, paper folding, matchsticks, a compass, and the nets and cards at the end of the book are on-screen versions that keep the book's rules. Offline-only items are the six on the image after Tool 1.
- **Chapter size:** the brief says no fixed number of tools. The chapter has 24 concepts, 20 activity boxes and 109 questions, so 22 tools and 46 rows is long. The user may prefer fewer (section 5).

Class 4 emphases and where they land: classification and comparison (Tools 5, 6, 15, 19); visual problem-solving (Tools 7, 8, 9, 20, 21, 22); patterns and relationships (F+V−E on Tool 3, double on Tool 17, sides equal angles on Tool 19); multi-step (Tool 17 folds, Tool 18 compass, Tool 3); measurement (Tools 16, 17, 18); investigations (Tools 3, 13, 14, 17); reasoning and application (Tools 8, 18, 21, 22).
