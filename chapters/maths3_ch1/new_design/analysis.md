## 2. Learning objectives the book is aiming at
These are read from what the tasks ask the child to do; the book prints no list of objectives.
1. Make one mark for each animal that leaves and strike out one mark for each that comes back, and say whether any is missing.
2. Count a long row of marks (47), and say how many came back when some marks are left over (45).
3. Make marks to keep track of two kinds of animals at once (36 cows and 23 sheep).
4. Count the letters in a name, and say which of several names is the longest and which is the shortest.
5. Compare two names by their letters and explain who has "a longer name" (Mahesh and Kartik).
6. Write friends' names; tick the longest, cross the shortest; name the starting letter of a name; count the names with the same starting letter or ending letter; find the most used starting letter; list the letters that start no name.
7. Make a number name from number cards (Forty + Three) and count its letters.
8. Write numbers between 1 and 99 whose names have 8 letters, and say what they have in common.
9. Find the number(s) with the longest name, solve two letter-count riddles ("Find out"), and make riddles of your own.
10. Read about the places in India with the longest and the shortest names.
11. Put picture cards and household objects into two groups by a rule; say what is common in a group; group the same objects in a different way.
12. Look at the children in the class, group them by hair style and count each group.

## 6. Printed-book errors and ambiguities
"Error" = the printing is wrong or contradicts itself. "Ambiguity" = it can be read more than one way. I fix nothing here.

### 6a. Errors
1. **Q2 on pdf 6 repeats the stem of Q1.** Both say "Write the number(s) between 1 and 99 that have the longest name." Q1 has three lines (73, 77, 78 fit exactly). Q2 holds two "Find out" riddles that do not ask for the longest name. The Q2 stem looks like a copy slip. ch1.letus6a quotes the stem as printed.
2. **"Some animals and birds" (pdf 3, Q1):** all ten cards are animals (tiger, dog, snake, goat, lion, elephant, deer, ox, rat, monkey); no bird is among them. (The book's own bird appears on pdf 6 and 7.)
3. **"What is common in each groups Jojo has made?" (pdf 7):** grammar slip ("each groups").

### 6b. Ambiguities
1. **Teji's grouping text is split across a page turn** (pdf 6 figure, pdf 7 sentence "She has grouped the cards into..."). The figure on pdf 6 has no label.
2. **ch1.fill1 depends on a picture count.** The number 47 is not printed. I counted it at 250 dpi and by program (20 + 20 + 7); the marks are drawn in groups of five with no slash, and the last group has two. A child who counts differently gets a different answer for ch1.fill2.
3. **ch1.letus2 (Mahesh, Kartik):** both names have 6 letters. By the book's own rule (pdf 3 Q1: longest name = most letters) neither is longer. In the thin handwriting font "Mahesh" is wider. The book gives no answer and says only "Discuss". The two boys share one bubble with two tails ("My name is longer."), and nothing says which boy is which.
4. **ch1.letus3e:** "Count the names with the same ending letter." The same as what? Part (c) says "as yours"; (e) does not. It could mean the names that end with the same letter as the child's own name, or the largest group of names ending with the same letter.
5. **ch1.letus3c, 3d:** whether the child's own name is among the eight lines is not said; ties for "the most used" starting letter are not covered; "the letters" in (f) means the letters of the alphabet (not said); only eight lines are given.
6. **ch1.letus6a "very near to 100":** the letter clues (two words, 6 then 5) fit 93, 97 and 98 below 100. "Very near" does not choose between them. The book asks "Who am I?" as if there were one.
7. **ch1.letus6b "the smallest among all my friends":** the intended meaning is the shortest number name (70, seventy, 7 letters). "Smallest" can also be read as the smallest number (63).
8. **ch1.letus8:** "Things that need electricity": TV, Lamp, Fan and Clock fit four lines; but a wall clock often runs on a battery, and a lamp may be an oil lamp. The four-and-four lines suggest the intended split.
9. **ch1.letus9 "the given objects":** it does not say which. After the household objects of pdf 7 and with four lines in each panel, eight objects fit; the ten picture cards of pdf 6 would need five lines each. The panels have no labels.
10. **ch1.letus10:** "Look at the children in your class." The six hair-style pictures have no names; the numbers come from the child's own class. The page also shows five unnamed children and Mala, which could be counted instead (my reading, not the book's instruction).
