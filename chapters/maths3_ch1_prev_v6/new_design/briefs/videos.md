## M1.S1.T1.C1.VID1  The Cows of Tarakeshwar (hook video, about 75 s)

- **Purpose:** Deba and Deep take the cows out every day and cannot count. How can they tell that no cow is lost? The story ends on that question: no idea, no number and no mark on the wall yet.
- **Concept it explains:** none (hook)
- **Characters:** Deba, Deep, their friend
- **Setting:** Tarakeshwar, a long time ago: the cow shed with its tiled roof and rough wooden gate, the green grazing fields, late evening light. Look of the cowherds, the friend, the shed and the cows as in the visual key (deba_and_deep, their_friend, cow_shed, cows).
- **What happens:** At sunset Deba and Deep bring the cows home. The cows crowd through the gate, push, stop to drink and mix together, so the herd looks like a moving crowd. Deba watches and cannot tell whether every cow has come home, and asks, 'How do we know that we have not lost any cows?' Deep looks at the crowd, then at the gate, and has no answer. Neither of them knows how to count. Their friend stands by the fence and listens, and says nothing yet. The story names no number and no solution, and no wall is shown.
- **Ends on the open question:** How can Deba and Deep tell that no cow is lost, when they do not know how to count?
- **Concept:** The Cows of Tarakeshwar (M1.S1.T1.C1)

## M1.S2.T2.C3.VID1  Marks Still on the Wall (concept video, about 115 s)

- **Purpose:** Years later the herd is large. The friend's idea is only recalled in one line, as the child did it on Tool 1. The cows come home, marks are still left, and the story shows that a mark left is a cow still out. No count is said: the marks, the cows out and the cows back are for Tool 2.
- **Concept it explains:** the marks left on the wall are the cows still out, and the cows that came back are the rest
- **Characters:** Deba, Deep, their friend
- **Setting:** Tarakeshwar, years later: the cow shed gate at evening and the big stone slab beside it, where the marks are kept in groups of five. The herd is much larger now (visual keys: stone_slab, tally_mark, struck_mark, cow_shed, cows, deba_and_deep).
- **What happens:** Years later the herd has grown. In one line, Deba reminds Deep of their friend's idea: a mark for each cow that goes out, and one struck out for each cow that comes back (the friend shows only in this quick memory). That evening the cows file in through the gate and Deep strikes out a mark for each one. The last cow walks in and the shed is full and quiet. But some marks on the slab are still standing, not struck out. Deba and Deep look at the slab and do not count the marks. Deba says a mark still standing is a cow still out, and they hurry out together to look for the cows. They find the cows in a nearby field, and all walk home together. The last marks are struck out. Deba ends with a thought: marks are one way to count without numbers, so is there another way? The story never says how many marks stand or how many cows are out, and shows no numeral anywhere.
- **The problem:** Every cow that came in has had its mark struck out, yet some marks are still standing. Deep thinks the marks left must be the cows that came home. Deba points at the gate: a cow that came home had its mark struck out. So what do the marks left stand for?
- **Story thread (how it continues):** Continues the hook and the child's try at the gate on Tool 1: Deba and Deep still use their friend's idea. It is recalled in one line only and is not taught again.
- **Continues from:** M1.S1.T1.C1.VID1
- **Leads into:** Tool 2, Marks for a Big Herd: the child reads the marks on the slab, strikes them out and finds the cows still out and the cows home.
- **Concept:** The Marks Left on the Wall (M1.S2.T2.C3)
